## Headline finding

Across all ten homepages there are **2,591 words total** — an average of **248 words per concept**.
A local service homepage that converts and ranks runs 800–1,500. Every concept is at a quarter to a
third of the depth the job needs.

| Metric | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Avg words per page | 248 |
| Thinnest (No. 06 Gallery First) | 102 |
| Richest (No. 07 Booking Led) | 363 |
| Unique testimonials across 10 pages | 5 |
| Concepts showing pricing | 2 / 10 |
| Concepts showing Shawna's face | 0 / 10 |

The look is in good shape. The content is thin, and the ten options are less different from each
other than they appear.

## Findings

### 1. Ten concepts, three architectures — *biggest issue*

Concepts 01–05 are the same website five times: hero → four service cards → gallery strip → one
testimonial → Instagram → closing CTA → footer. Identical order, identical band count,
near-identical word count. They differ in palette and typeface only.

Shawna is being told she has ten choices while actually choosing between three structures — the
card grid (01–05), the booking funnel (07), and the long-form story (08, 09, 10) — with 06 as a
stripped gallery outlier. She will spend the call picking a colour and believe she picked a website.

**Direction:** collapse to three fully built architectures; present palette as a separate, explicit
choice. Ten thin options is worse for her than three complete ones.

### 2. The copy does not vary, so she cannot react to it

The same H1 — *"Celebrate life and make our time stand still."* — is the headline in six concepts
and the quoted tagline in the other four plus all ten footers. Five testimonials cover ten pages:
Megan R. in 01/06/09; Tasha & Rob in 03/07/08; Daniel K. in 02/10.

Because the words are constant, every reaction will be about colour. There is no way for her to say
"that one sounds like me."

**Direction:** give each architecture a distinct voice — own headline, sub-headline and CTA wording
— and note the voice in the concept label. Let her choose voice and look independently.

### 3. Placeholder facts are wrong and contradictory — *fix before she sees it*

| In the mockups | Actual |
| --- | --- |
| (815) 555-0142 | 815.715.8647 |
| hello@skybyrdphoto.com | ShawnaL@SkyByrdPhotography.com |
| skybyrdphoto.com | skybyrdphotography.com |
| "Plainfield, Illinois" only | Boutique lifestyle photographer serving the Chicago suburbs |
| "since 2016" (03) vs "since 2014" (08, 09, 10) | Pick one — the mockups contradict each other |

Also invented and presented as fact: the price list ($450 / $525 / $680 / $275 in 07 and 10),
"300+ sessions to date" (04), gallery counts 142/88/51/37 (06), "10 yrs archived" (10), and five
named clients with quotes. The prices will be read as a proposal.

**Direction:** replace real contact details everywhere; give anything still standing in a visible
treatment at the point of the claim. The note buried in the Questions panel is not enough.

### 4. The Instagram embed is eating the page

Nine of ten concepts end with a grid of empty Instagram tiles — 48 of them in concepts 08 and 10.
In a 248-word page, an empty embed is often the largest block on screen, and it pushes the closing
CTA far below the fold.

**Direction:** cut to a single row of six, move it below the CTA and above the footer, populate with
real posts.

### 5. There is no mobile view

Every artboard is desktop. Most traffic to a local portrait photographer arrives on a phone, usually
from Instagram. Layouts depending on a fixed sidebar (06), a wide masthead (09) or a sticky chapter
index (10) behave very differently at 390px — and those are three of the four distinct structures.

**Direction:** a mobile artboard beside each architecture. If only one view per concept, show the phone.

## Twenty content blocks to add

### Earn trust

1. **Meet Shawna — with a photograph of her.** *Highest impact.* No concept shows the photographer's
   face. People hire a person, not a studio. Include a real portrait, 120–180 words first person,
   and the detail that is actually her brand — mom, wife, Nonna, which is why family work is what
   she does. Sign it. Top third of the page.
2. **Social proof at volume, with sources.** *Highest impact.* One testimonial is decoration. She has
   real listings on Google, Facebook and Yelp going unused. Rating + review count + platform badges
   near the top, then 6–12 real quotes with first name, session type and town. One relevant quote
   inside each session-type block.
3. **Credentials and track record.** Years shooting, sessions delivered, insured, memberships, local
   awards, features. A slim four-figure trust bar under the hero. Settle 2014-vs-2016 first.
4. **What a session actually feels like.** Concepts 07/08/10 have the best writing in the set here
   ("the first ten minutes are throwaway frames on purpose"). Seven concepts have none of it. This
   answers the main reason people delay booking. Promote the walkthrough into every architecture.

### Convert

5. **Pricing, or at least a starting price.** *Highest impact.* Only 07 and 10 show any. Hiding price
   changes who enquires, not how many. Four-column comparison — duration, group size, locations,
   image count, delivery, price. "Sessions from $X" is the minimum. Get real figures first.
6. **What you actually receive.** Image count, turnaround, gallery delivery and lifetime, print
   release, retouching policy, raw file availability. Only 10 gestures at it.
7. **A real enquiry form.** Every concept has a "Book a Session" button; none shows what happens
   after the click. Session type, date range, group size, location preference, free text. Plus 07's
   reassurance — no deposit to enquire, reply within a day.
8. **Session types as real blocks, not cards.** "Treats allowed. Chaos expected." is charming and
   tells a buyer nothing. Per type: who it suits, duration, inclusions, price, 3–4 images, a matching
   testimonial, own CTA. This alone roughly doubles the page.
9. **A real FAQ.** Only 07 has one, with three questions. Highest-leverage block on the page — removes
   booking friction, cuts email load, and is what search engines and AI assistants quote. Target
   8–12: what to wear · how long · rain · travel · deposit and cancellation · image count · turnaround
   · grandparents · pets on location · team headshots · prints · rescheduling.
10. **Current availability and seasonal deadlines.** Portrait work is deadline-driven and nothing uses
    it. 07's "3 dates left in May" is the only nod. Add fall family, holiday card deadlines, senior
    class-of cut-offs, spring minis.

### Get found

11. **Service area, named town by town.** *Highest impact.* Every concept says "Plainfield, Illinois"
    and stops. Her actual positioning is the Chicago suburbs. This is the biggest single miss against
    the goal of bringing in additional clients — someone in Naperville will never reach a page that
    only says Plainfield. Name Plainfield, Naperville, Joliet, Oswego, Shorewood, Yorkville,
    Romeoville, Bolingbrook, in real sentences rather than a keyword list.
12. **Favourite locations, with photographs.** Concept 09 has the instinct — "DuPage riverfront," "the
    bakery on Lockport St." Specific places persuade better than adjectives and rank for searches
    nobody else targets. 4–6 real locations, each with image, what it suits, best season.
13. **A journal that exists.** Five concepts link "Journal" in the footer; no concept contains one. A
    footer link to nothing is worse than no link. Either three recent-session posts feeding a real
    journal, or remove the link everywhere.
14. **A preparation guide.** "What should we wear" is the most-asked and most-searched question in
    portrait photography. 07 mentions a guide sent after booking — putting it on the site earns
    traffic instead of just answering email.

### Earn more per client

15. **Prints, albums and wall art.** Every concept treats the digital gallery as the end product.
    Print sales are where studio revenue per client comes from, and framed work on a real wall is the
    most photogenic thing on a photography site.
16. **Gift certificates.** Absent from all ten. Low effort, buys directly from the page, turns clients
    into a referral channel. Strong around holidays and new babies.
17. **Email capture.** Several concepts say sessions open two months out — so a real share of visitors
    cannot book today and there is no way to hold them. No email capture anywhere in the set.
18. **Client gallery access.** Returning clients need somewhere to go; its absence makes an established
    studio look brand new.
19. **Thirty seconds of video.** A clip of a session in progress sells the experience better than any
    copy here, and directly answers the "I'm awkward on camera" objection.
20. **Referral and repeat-client offer.** Family portraits are inherently recurring. Nothing in the set
    speaks to someone who has already booked once.

## Recommended homepage order

Fifteen bands, roughly 1,100–1,400 words. **Bold** rows do not exist in any of the ten concepts.
The sequence answers questions in the order visitors ask them: *is this any good · who are you ·
what do you do · can I afford it · what happens · do others trust you · will you come to me · when ·
what if · then what.*

| # | Band | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Sticky nav | Logo, four links, phone number, booking button |
| 02 | Hero | Headline, one-sentence sub, two CTAs, one strong photograph. Keep the tagline |
| 03 | **Trust bar** | Rating, review count, years, sessions, service area |
| 04 | **Meet Shawna** | Photograph, 120–180 words, signed. Highest-value addition |
| 05 | Session types | Four full blocks with inclusions, price, images, quote, CTA |
| 06 | Featured gallery | Filterable. The concepts already do this well |
| 07 | **What you receive** | Image count, turnaround, gallery life, print release, retouching |
| 08 | How it works | Five steps. Promote concept 08's writing |
| 09 | **Reviews** | 6–12 real quotes with names, session types, source badges |
| 10 | **Where I shoot** | Named towns, 4–6 locations with images. Main search lever |
| 11 | **Right now** | Current availability and this month's seasonal deadline |
| 12 | **FAQ** | 8–12 questions, expandable |
| 13 | **Prints and gift cards** | Framed work on a wall, albums, gift certificates |
| 14 | Enquiry | The real form, plus email capture |
| 15 | Footer | Deep links, full contact, hours, socials, client login, service area |

Instagram: one row of six, between 14 and 15. Not a 48-tile grid between the reader and the CTA.

## Brief for the next round

- **Ask Shawna for the real content first.** Prices, bio and portrait, her actual reviews, image
  library, preferred contact details. The next round should contain almost no invented facts — that
  is the difference between a mockup she can approve and one she can only react to.
- **Three architectures, not ten.** Card grid (best of 01–05), booking funnel (07), long-form story
  (10, with 08's letter voice and 09's specificity). Build each to the full fifteen-band spine.
- **Palette as a separate choice.** Four skins — warm linen, dark studio, bright modern, soft and airy
  — applied to the same architecture, so the two decisions are genuinely independent. The Questions
  panel already promises this; make it a control rather than an offer.
- **Distinct copy per architecture,** so she can pick a voice.
- **Desktop and mobile for each.** Six artboards. If forced to choose, show the phone.
- **Mark every placeholder visibly,** at the point of the claim.
- **Keep what is working:** the tagline, the photography-forward layouts, the "first ten minutes are
  throwaways" writing, concept 09's specificity, concept 07's booking flow. The craft is not the
  problem — the depth is.

**One-line version for the design call:** three complete websites she can choose a business from,
instead of ten colour studies she can only choose a mood from — and roughly four times the content
on each.
